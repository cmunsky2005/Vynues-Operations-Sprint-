# Escalation Paths

**Sprint:** Operations Sprint — Quality Assurance, Supply Chains & Procurement  
**Owner:** COO / Operations Team  
**Review Cadence:** Quarterly

---

# Purpose

Escalation Paths establish a standardized process for resolving operational issues that cannot be addressed through normal procedures. A consistent escalation process supports timely decision-making, operational continuity, and continuous improvement.

---

# Scope

Escalation procedures apply to:

- Vendor onboarding
- Procurement processes
- Supply chain operations
- Quality assurance activities
- Vendor performance issues
- Operational process failures

---

# Escalation Levels

| Level | Responsible Party | Responsibility |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Level 1** | Operational Owner | Resolve routine operational issues and document corrective actions. |
| **Level 2** | Department Manager | Resolve operational issues affecting workflow performance or requiring additional resources. |
| **Level 3** | Chief Operating Officer (COO) | Resolve critical operational issues affecting business operations, vendor relationships, or service delivery. |

---

# Escalation Triggers

Issues should be escalated when:

- A critical operational workflow cannot be completed.
- A Service Level Agreement (SLA) cannot be met.
- A vendor issue remains unresolved.
- A procurement process is blocked.
- A supply chain disruption affects operations.
- A corrective action does not resolve the issue.

---

# Escalation Process

```text
Operational Issue Identified
          ↓
Attempt Resolution
          ↓
Issue Resolved?
      ↓          ↓
    Yes          No
     ↓            ↓
Document      Escalate to
Resolution    Next Level
```

---

# Resolution

After an issue has been resolved:

- Document the corrective action.
- Update operational documentation if necessary.
- Review the issue during the next operational review.
- Incorporate lessons learned into the Continuous Improvement Process (T8).
