SCOR Framework Applied to Vynues

SCOR Explanation: A globally recognized framework developed by the Association for Supply Chain Management. It helps companies design, measure, analyze, and improve supply chains using a common language and process. The core supply chain processes are Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, and Return. This process should result in ongoing improvement rather than one-time fixes — organizations should measure results, analyze performance, improve weak processes, and repeat the cycle as operations evolve.

Plan
The first step of the SCOR model is planning. Planning activities include forecasting demand, capacity planning, scheduling, resource allocation, and risk planning. For Vynues, planning might include figuring out the fastest and most efficient way someone can book an event through the app. For example, when a client submits an event request, the Vynues app needs to make sure the right venues and vendors are available on that specific date, determine whether the date falls during a high-demand season, confirm that the inventory needed for the event is booked in advance, and ensure the event budget is scoped out and internally approved.

Key Inputs
* Gathering all of the client's needed information
* Historical booking data
* Vendor availability calendars
  
Key Outputs
* Event requirement checklist
* Vendor shortlist
* Internal capacity flag (can we do this or not?)
  
Risk
* Double-booking a venue
* Underestimating the lead time for a vendor
* No backup plan if the first-choice venue is unavailable

Source
The second step of SCOR is sourcing, which includes selecting vendors, purchasing, contract management, vendor qualification, and receiving goods or services. This is where venues and vendors are searched and shortlisted, vendor vetting happens, quotes are collected and compared, contracts are signed, and purchase orders or booking deposits are issued.

Examples include:
* Venue: Ballrooms, rooftops, conference centers
* Catering: Catering companies, food trucks
* AV and Tech: Sound systems, lighting, live streaming
* Staffing: Event staff, security, hosts
* Logistics: Transportation, furniture rental, floral arrangements

Key Outputs
* Signed vendor contracts with SLAs
* Confirmed bookings
* Vendor contact sheet added to event file

Things that can go wrong
* A vendor doesn't have liability insurance, exposing Vynues to risk
* Contract terms are vague, causing disputes later
* Vendor cancels last minute with no penalty clause

Make
The third step of SCOR is "Make" — for Vynues, this is the event preparation and staging phase: turning contracts into real, coordinated events. This includes producing a run-of-show document, which is a minute-by-minute timeline of everything that happens. Vendors are briefed on logistics: load-in times, setup specs, and point of contact. A site walkthrough happens (or a virtual check-in for remote events). Internal QA checks are run to confirm everything is approved and that a backup plan exists, and lastly, the client completes a pre-event review/approval.

Key documents produced during this process
* Run-of-show
* Vendor briefing packets
* Floor plan/layout diagram
* Emergency contact sheet
* Equipment checklist

Things that can go wrong
* Vendor receives wrong briefing information (wrong address, wrong time)
* Setup time isn't coordinated (e.g., the AV crew arrives while the caterer is still loading in)
* Client changes requirements at the last minute and vendors aren't notified

Deliver
The fourth step of SCOR is delivering, where clients get what they were promised from the beginning. During this process, vendors execute their contracted scope (setup, service, breakdown); an on-site coordinator organizes the delivery and handles real-time issue resolution; guest/attendee experience is monitored; and the event is documented (photos, incident log, timeline deviations).

Key Processes Tracked
Metric| What It Measures
On-time setup completion| Did everything start at the scheduled time?
Vendor no-show rate| Did anyone fail to show up?
Incident count| How many problems occurred during the event?
Client satisfaction| Post-event survey score
SLA adherence rate| Did vendors meet their contracted standards?

Things that can go wrong
* A vendor no-shows
* Client requests a change mid-event that costs extra
* A safety incident occurs
  
Return
The last step of the SCOR process is Return, which means handling things that didn't go right. On the client side, this involves: the client submits a complaint or dispute (e.g., the caterer was two hours late); a refund or partial credit is assessed; a formal post-event debrief is conducted; and a client satisfaction survey is collected. On the vendor side: vendor invoices are reviewed and approved or disputed; if a vendor underperformed, a performance flag is added to their profile; the vendor is placed on a watch list or removed from the approved list; and equipment returns or venue damage deposits are processed.

Key Outputs
* Closed-loop complaint ticket
* Vendor performance score updated
* Root cause documented
* Any contract disputes escalated to legal if needed

Things that can go wrong
* No formal dispute process — the client leaves a bad review and leaves
* Vendor performance issues aren't recorded, so the same bad vendor gets hired again
* No root cause analysis, so the same problem happens at the next ten events

Three Quick Recommendations for Vynues
* Build an approved vendor registry — Don't source from scratch every time. Maintain a pre-vetted list with insurance status, ratings, and contract templates ready to go. This cuts Source time dramatically.
  
* Standardize the run-of-show template — Every event should use the same format so any coordinator can pick it up. This reduces errors in Make.
  
* Close the loop from Return to Plan — After every event, the three biggest issues should be logged and reviewed before the next event brief is written. This is how you actually improve over time.
