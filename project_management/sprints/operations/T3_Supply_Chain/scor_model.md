# SCOR Framework Applied to Vynues

## SCOR Overview

The **Supply Chain Operations Reference (SCOR)** model is a globally recognized framework developed by the Association for Supply Chain Management (ASCM). It provides organizations with a standardized approach to designing, measuring, analyzing, and improving supply chain performance. The framework organizes supply chain activities into five core processes: **Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, and Return**.

For Vynues, the SCOR framework provides a structured method for managing the entire event lifecycle—from the moment a client submits an event request through post-event evaluation and continuous improvement. Rather than serving as a one-time process, SCOR is designed as a continuous improvement cycle in which operational performance is measured, weaknesses are analyzed, corrective actions are implemented, and lessons learned are incorporated into future events.

---

# Plan

The **Plan** phase establishes the operational foundation for every event. Planning activities include demand forecasting, capacity planning, scheduling, resource allocation, budgeting, and risk assessment.

For Vynues, planning begins when a client submits an event request through the platform. Operations teams determine venue and vendor availability, evaluate seasonal demand, estimate resource requirements, verify inventory availability, and ensure the proposed event aligns with budget and operational capacity before moving forward.

### Key Inputs

* Client event requirements
* Historical booking data
* Vendor availability calendars
* Venue availability
* Budget estimates

### Key Outputs

* Event requirements checklist
* Vendor shortlist
* Internal capacity assessment
* Preliminary event plan
* Risk assessment

### Risks

* Double-booking a venue
* Underestimating vendor lead times
* Insufficient operational capacity
* No contingency plan if preferred vendors or venues become unavailable

---

# Source

The **Source** phase focuses on acquiring the vendors and services required to execute an event. Activities include vendor identification, supplier evaluation, contract negotiation, procurement, vendor qualification, and purchase authorization.

Vynues identifies qualified venues and vendors, compares pricing and proposals, verifies insurance and certifications, negotiates agreements, and executes contracts before confirming reservations and issuing deposits.

### Examples of Sourced Services

* **Venues:** Ballrooms, rooftops, conference centers
* **Catering:** Catering companies, food trucks
* **Audio/Visual:** Sound systems, lighting, live streaming
* **Staffing:** Event staff, security personnel, hosts
* **Logistics:** Transportation, furniture rental, floral arrangements

### Key Outputs

* Signed vendor contracts
* Service Level Agreements (SLAs)
* Confirmed venue and vendor bookings
* Vendor contact information
* Procurement documentation

### Risks

* Vendors lacking required insurance or certifications
* Poorly written contracts leading to disputes
* Vendor cancellations without penalty clauses
* Selecting unqualified vendors

---

# Make

Within Vynues' service-based supply chain, the **Make** phase represents event preparation and operational readiness rather than manufacturing. This stage transforms signed agreements into a coordinated event execution plan.

Activities include developing the run-of-show, preparing venue layouts, coordinating vendor logistics, conducting site walkthroughs, completing quality assurance reviews, and obtaining final client approval.

### Key Documents Produced

* Run-of-show
* Vendor briefing packets
* Floor plan and layout diagrams
* Emergency contact sheet
* Equipment checklist
* Quality assurance checklist

### Risks

* Vendors receiving incorrect logistical information
* Poor coordination between vendors during setup
* Last-minute client changes not communicated effectively
* Incomplete quality assurance inspections

---

# Deliver

The **Deliver** phase represents live event execution. During this stage, vendors fulfill their contractual responsibilities while Vynues coordinates operations, manages customer communication, and resolves issues in real time.

Activities include overseeing setup, monitoring vendor performance, managing attendee experience, documenting operational issues, and ensuring contractual obligations are fulfilled throughout the event.

### Key Performance Metrics

| Metric                    | What It Measures                              |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| On-Time Setup Completion  | Percentage of setups completed on schedule    |
| Vendor No-Show Rate       | Frequency of vendor attendance failures       |
| Incident Count            | Number of operational issues during the event |
| Client Satisfaction Score | Post-event customer survey results            |
| SLA Compliance Rate       | Vendor compliance with contractual standards  |

### Risks

* Vendor no-shows
* Unexpected client requests during the event
* Safety incidents
* Equipment failures
* Event delays

---

# Return

The **Return** phase focuses on post-event evaluation, issue resolution, and continuous improvement. Unlike traditional supply chains, Return within Vynues emphasizes customer service recovery, vendor performance management, and operational learning.

Client-related activities include reviewing complaints, issuing refunds or credits when appropriate, conducting post-event meetings, and collecting customer feedback.

Vendor-related activities include reviewing invoices, evaluating performance, updating vendor scorecards, processing equipment returns or venue deposits, and documenting corrective actions when necessary.

### Key Outputs

* Closed complaint tickets
* Updated vendor performance scores
* Root cause analysis documentation
* Customer satisfaction reports
* Corrective action recommendations
* Escalated legal or contractual disputes when required

### Risks

* Lack of a formal complaint resolution process
* Failure to document poor vendor performance
* Repeated operational issues due to missing root cause analysis
* Customer dissatisfaction leading to reputational damage

# Full Supply Chain Summary

| SCOR Phase  | Primary Owner                 | Key Outputs                                                   | Primary Risks                                              |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Plan**    | Operations & Sales            | Event brief, vendor shortlist, capacity assessment            | Double-booking, inaccurate forecasting, missed lead times  |
| **Source**  | Procurement                   | Signed contracts, confirmed vendors, SLAs                     | Unqualified vendors, weak contracts, vendor cancellations  |
| **Make**    | Operations & Vendors          | Run-of-show, briefing packets, QA approval                    | Miscommunication, setup delays, incomplete preparation     |
| **Deliver** | On-Site Coordinator & Vendors | Successful event execution, incident log, customer experience | Vendor no-shows, safety incidents, service disruptions     |
| **Return**  | Client Success & Procurement  | Complaint resolution, vendor scorecards, improvement plans    | Poor follow-up, recurring issues, customer dissatisfaction |

# Recommendations for Vynues

### 1. Develop an Approved Vendor Registry

Maintain a centralized database of pre-qualified vendors that includes insurance status, certifications, performance ratings, pricing history, and standardized contract templates. This will reduce sourcing time, improve procurement consistency, and lower operational risk.

### 2. Standardize Event Documentation

Adopt standardized templates for run-of-show documents, vendor briefing packets, equipment checklists, and quality assurance inspections. Consistent documentation reduces communication errors and allows coordinators to manage events more efficiently.

### 3. Create a Continuous Improvement Loop

Establish a formal post-event review process in which operational metrics, customer feedback, vendor performance, and incident reports are analyzed after every event. Lessons learned should be incorporated into future planning, procurement decisions, and quality assurance processes to strengthen long-term operational performance.
